Atlas Explorer

About

Atlas Explorer is an interactive web catalog of electronic atlases that enables you to search for atlases, explore their structure, and conduct your own comparative and trend analyses. The aim of the Atlas Explorer project is to identify, analyze, classify, and archive electronic atlases in order to preserve, systematize, and expand knowledge about atlases and their diverse metaconcepts.

Atlas Explorer classifies electronic atlases by bibliographic, content, and media-cartographic attributes organised into five blocks: general information, interface, information architecture (IA) and navigation, content representation, and functionality. Definitions of all attributes are available in the table header tooltips.

Methodological aspects of analysis, glossary and other useful information can be found in the Supplementary Materials section. Unlike the time-consuming standard analysis model, Atlas Explorer employs an express analysis approach that usually takes 2–3 hours. While this approach enables the identification of the most important and synthetic attributes, it provides lower analytical depth and reliability compared to the standard model.

New atlases and attributes are forthcoming, and your suggestions are welcome!

Enjoy exploring the atlases!

Atlas Criteria

  1. Defined theme and territory
  2. Includes multiple thematic sections or ready-made thematic maps (excluding the metaconcept of organizational mechanisms)
  3. Includes purpose-built maps or (geo)spatial visualisations
  4. Content is delivered either within the atlas shell or on purpose-built pages (not PDF pages)
  5. Operational and completed product (not a beta version)
  6. Not a catalog geoportal, collection of dashboards, interactive map, (web)GIS or applied CIS

Electronic Atlases: Development Trends and Research Methodology

Book Published: December 30, 2025

The book presents a periodization of electronic atlas (EA) development and identifies metaconcepts of atlas applications. It refines the classification of EAs, clarifies the concept of the EA, and outlines current research approaches and methods. The book provides a detailed characterization of four core EA components as end-user applications: interface, information architecture and navigation, content representation, and functionality. It also introduces and validates a methodology for media-cartographic analysis of EAs through empirical case studies of the European Atlas of the Seas and the Czech Historical Atlas.

Revisiting the Electronic Atlas Concept: From Theoretical Model to Metaconcepts of Atlas Applications

Article Published: June 15, 2026

The article introduces the concept of ‘atlas metaconcept’ and proposes a framework for describing the metaconcepts of electronic atlases. It develops a typology of nine metaconcepts and a matrix of indicators for their identification based on a media-cartographic analysis of 100 electronic atlases. Each metaconcept is accompanied by a definition, a brief description, and examples of atlases.
